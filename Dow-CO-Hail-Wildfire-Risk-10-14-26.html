Every populated Colorado ZIP code scored for hail and wildfire, with strong wind, winter weather and tornado as smaller inputs, from the FEMA National Risk Index (December 2025 release) at census-tract resolution, weighted to where each ZIP's residents live, and grouped into four tiers. Hail is the hazard nearly every Front Range home shares; wildfire is what sets the foothill, mountain and grassland-edge ZIPs apart. Section 04 is the recommended treatment for the Home campaign, section 05 covers the Front Range around Castle Rock, and section 06 matches the tiers against the 855 leads GOAL has delivered since May 1. Scoring system

Public federal data at census-tract resolution

What goes into the score
  • HailNRI hail risk: how often damaging hail falls times the building value exposed, scored 0 to 100 nationally. Half of Colorado housing sits in ZIPs scoring above 93.5.
  • WildfireNRI wildfire risk: modeled burn likelihood and intensity times the value exposed. The tier rules also use the share of a ZIP's residents in tracts FEMA rates Relatively High or Very High for wildfire, which picks out the wildland-urban edge.
  • Smaller inputsNRI strong wind, winter weather and tornado risk: added to the composite score at lower weight; shown in the spreadsheet.
  • AvailabilityCarrier models (roof condition, defensible space, distance to hydrant) are property-level and not published by ZIP. This composite uses public federal data on the same hazards.

Tier rules
  • ExtremeHail score 98.5+, or 50%+ of the ZIP's residents in tracts rated Relatively High or Very High for wildfire
  • HighHail score 97.5+, 20%+ of residents in high-wildfire tracts, or composite score 88+
  • ModerateHail score 93+, 5%+ of residents in high-wildfire tracts, or composite score 78+
  • LowEverything else. Because almost all of Colorado is above the national median for hail, the cut points are set within Colorado.

Home campaign: recommended treatment

Extreme tier checked against Allstate appetite · High tier gets a roof and deductible review

Check appetite · Extreme · 220 ZIPs · 18.9% of CO housing Roof and deductible review · High · 97 ZIPs · 29.3% No change · 195 ZIPs · 51.8%
Excluding every Extreme ZIP would remove 18.9% of Colorado's housing from the Home campaign; adding the High tier would bring it to 48.2%, including Castle Rock itself. We do not recommend a blanket exclusion. We recommend excluding only the ZIPs where Allstate is not writing new home business, and treating the rest of the Extreme and High tiers as a conversation: roof age and material, the wind and hail deductible, and wildfire questions where they apply.
Home treatment by tier
  • ExtremeCheck appetite. Confirm Allstate is writing new home business in the ZIP before the first call. On every quote, ask roof age and material, explain the wind and hail deductible, and in wildfire ZIPs ask about defensible space and access roads.
  • HighRoof and deductible review. Ask the year the roof was replaced and whether it is impact-resistant, and walk through the wind and hail deductible before the first spring storm.
  • ModerateNo change. Standard quote; roof age is still worth asking along the Front Range.
  • LowNo change. Standard quote.
Two kinds of Extreme ZIP
  • Hail89 ZIPs reach Extreme on hail (score 98.5+), holding 232,448 housing units: Eastern Plains 46, Denver Metro 26, Northern Front Range 14, Pueblo & Southern Colorado 2, Mountain & Resort Counties 1.
  • Wildfire148 ZIPs reach Extreme on wildfire (half or more of residents in high-wildfire tracts), holding 268,487 housing units: Western Slope 37, Colorado Springs & Pikes Peak 24, Northern Front Range 22, Pueblo & Southern Colorado 22, Denver Metro 21, Mountain & Resort Counties 17, Eastern Plains 5.
  • Both17 ZIPs meet both rules.

Wildfire and hail in the conversation

Questions that make a Colorado home quote faster and stickier

Why it matters here
  • WildfireIn wildfire ZIPs, ask about defensible space, roof and siding material, and road access on the first call. Having the answers ready keeps the quote moving and shows the client you know their area.
  • Roofs86% of housing in the Denver Metro, Northern Front Range and Colorado Springs regions sits in ZIPs scoring 90+ for hail nationally. Roof age and whether the roof is impact-resistant belong in the first conversation, not the renewal.
  • DeductiblesIn High and Extreme ZIPs the wind and hail deductible is worth explaining at quote time, so there are no surprises after a storm.
  • AutoHail damage to a car is paid under comprehensive coverage. On every Auto quote in a hail ZIP, confirm comprehensive is included; it is also the natural opening to quote the home.

Data & files

  • Risk sourceFEMA National Risk Index, census tract table, December 2025 release (hail, wildfire, strong wind, winter weather, tornado), all 1,447 Colorado tracts.
  • GeographyU.S. Census Bureau 2020 ZIP Code Tabulation Areas and the 2020 ZCTA-to-tract relationship file. The file reports land-area overlaps, not population, so each tract's values are weighted into a ZIP by the tract's population times the share of its land inside the ZIP. Tract parts outside Colorado are not scored. Housing counts from ACS 2020–2024.
  • LeadsGOAL prospects for this account, counted by ZIP. 483 of 483 Home leads and 371 of 372 Auto leads matched a populated Colorado ZIP; the other used a PO-box ZIP (80932).
  • LimitsA ZIP score describes the ZIP, not a house. Roof condition, construction and defensible space decide each quote; the tiers decide where to ask first.
  • FilesDow-CO-ZIP-Data-10-14-26.csv · every ZIP, every score, tier and treatment column, plus the demographics used in the companion report.
